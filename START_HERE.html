Eight things to know

  1. Unzip the folder first

    Extract the whole zip before you open anything (Windows: right-click it and choose Extract All; macOS: double-click it). Keep the folders as they are: the pages link to each other and to the assets folder.

  2. Open the course home

    Open index.html in the extracted folder. It has the syllabus, the fifteen-week schedule and a link to every module's slides, every lab, the capstone, the reflections and the paper critiques.

  3. In week 1: set up Python

    The labs need Python 3.11 or newer with NumPy, on your own computer or in a free Google Colab or GitHub Codespaces account. No paid software is needed. The labs page (labs/index.html) has links to catch up on Python, and the data for every lab is in labs/data, explained in labs/data/README.md. Before Lab 5, make its image data once: in labs/data/lab5, run python3 make_dataset.py (a few seconds; it builds the same file for everyone).

  4. Every week

    Read the module's slides (weeks/weekNN/index.html; press P in a deck to save it as a PDF) and the reading on the schedule. Section 01 meets on Fridays, 8:30 to 11:10 a.m. CT, at the Missouri Innovation Campus (MIC); the room will be announced. Section 02 follows the recorded lecture posted for the week. Both sections post to the week's discussion, which closes on Friday.

  5. Submitting work

    Submit labs, reflections, critiques and capstone work through the course management system. Each lab sheet says what to hand in and how to check your own output before you submit. Read the course's policy on AI tools on the course home before lab 1: use is allowed for some tasks and must always be disclosed.

  6. Deadlines

    Sunday, 11:59 p.m. Central, unless the assignment says otherwise. Labs, reflections, critiques and the capstone proposal and checkpoint lose 10% per day for up to three days, then are not accepted. There are no free late days. The midterm, the capstone report and the capstone presentation have fixed dates: if one is impossible for you, contact Dr. Kar before it.

  7. Midterm

    Friday, March 5, 8:30 to 10:30 a.m. CT, on modules 1 to 7. Section 01 sits it at MIC; Section 02 sits the same paper online, proctored. Allowed: one handwritten 8.5 × 11 in note card, both sides, and the formula sheet handed out with the exam. No calculators. A practice midterm with answers is in weeks/week08/practice.html.

  8. Capstone

    The capstone runs through the second half of the term, with a proposal, a checkpoint, a report and a presentation in class during finals week, May 3 to 7. The capstone pages (capstone/index.html) have each milestone's requirements.

Using the slides

In the slides, use the arrow keys or the Previous and Next buttons to move. Press O, or the Slides button, to see every slide at once and jump to any of them. R replays a slide's animation and P prints or saves as PDF. On a phone, turn it sideways for larger slides. Every page opens with a plain double-click; nothing needs a server.

Getting help

Office hours: Tuesday and Wednesday, 12:00 to 3:00 p.m., MIC room D159. Questions about a lab: post in the course discussion, so classmates can see the answer, or email kar@ucmo.edu.
